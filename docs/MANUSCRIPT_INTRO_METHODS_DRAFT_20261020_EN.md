## Introduction

Blood transcriptomics offers a practical way to study systemic changes associated with Parkinson's disease (PD), but discrimination within a development cohort does not establish that a classifier will work in a different cohort or on a different assay. Age, sex, blood-cell composition, and sample processing can all alter measured expression. A model may therefore separate cases from controls by relying on features associated with the cohort or its technical workflow, even when its cross-validation score appears promising.

Dayan and colleagues evaluated 13 previously published blood-transcriptomic signatures in the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI) and in a small prospective cohort. Their simplified direction-based scoring yielded a median PPMI AUROC of 0.597 among the signatures with statistically significant discrimination, below the performance reported in the original studies. That work established an important external-replication benchmark, but its primary question concerned the behavior of *published signatures*, rather than the incremental value of a newly trained gene block beyond demographic and blood-cell-marker predictors under a fixed cohort-transfer design. [Dayan et al., *npj Parkinson's Disease*, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41531-025-01238-y).

We ask whether a gene-augmented blood-expression model developed in two public microarray cohorts improves discrimination over the *same model without its gene block* when transported to an independent RNA-sequencing cohort. We compare five prespecified models in within-cohort nested cross-validation, in both directions of transfer between the development cohorts, and ultimately in PPMI after a second public lock. Six fixed expression-derived cell-marker scores provide a common baseline for the primary M5-versus-M4 comparison. Technical-metadata diagnostics and bounded sensitivity analyses assess how much of the development result may depend on demographic imbalance, overlapping panel genes, or batch structure. These analyses address predictive transport of this particular procedure; they cannot assign a causal fraction of its signal to PD biology, cellular mixture, or technical effects.

## Methods

### Study design and data sources

The two GEO development cohorts were PROBE (GSE57475; 93 PD, 49 controls) and GENEPARK-TRO (GSE99039; 120 idiopathic PD, 119 controls with complete age and recorded sex), giving 381 development records. The reserved external cohort is PPMI Project 133 IR3/B38 at baseline, restricted to Long RNA, valid quality control, valid age and sex, and concordant historical PD or healthy-control labels. Linkage of release records to count files produced 472 *provisional* baseline candidates (311 PD, 161 controls). The final population and exclusion flow, including pre-QC group denominators, must be fixed and hashed before the second public lock. PPMI group metadata were viewed for feasibility and linkage before scoring, as disclosed in the registration; no PPMI expression-derived model or control score had been used in the analyses described here.

### Expression representation and marker scores

The frozen common universe contains 16,364 protein-coding genes with unique Entrez-to-GENCODE v29 mapping. For microarrays, valid probes for a gene were reduced to their within-sample median. For PPMI RNA-seq, featureCounts are divided by fixed reference exon-union lengths. Both inputs are then converted within each sample to fractional midranks, `(midrank - 0.5) / G`, over the common gene universe. This transformation uses no other sample from the external cohort. Six fixed panel scores are the means of those ranks over marker lists for T, B, monocytic, neutrophil, erythroid, and platelet signals. They are expression-derived proxies, not measured cell fractions. Gene filtering by positive median absolute deviation, selection of at most 1,000 genes, and standardization are refitted within every training partition; the held-out partition does not contribute to these operations.

### Models, fitting, and selection

All five PD models use a binomial-logit objective. M1 includes standardized age, age squared, recorded sex, and an age-by-sex interaction. M2 has an intercept and a selected gene block. M3 adds that gene block to M1. M4 adds the six fixed cell-marker scores to M1. M5 adds the selected gene block to the *identical M4 baseline*. Baseline coefficients and intercepts are unpenalized; only gene coefficients receive elastic-net penalties. The registered gene grid is lambda `{0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1}` and rho `{0, 0.5, 1}`, with a no-gene candidate for M3 and M5. Ties within 1e-6 favor no genes, then larger lambda and larger rho. A selected nonzero gene block at a grid boundary triggers at most two one-decade extensions; persistent boundary selections are retained and reported. A no-gene choice means that the gene block was *not selected*, rather than that a zero biological effect was demonstrated.

Each M1–M5 fit uses training-only source-by-class weights `n/(2 K n[source,class])`, where `K` is the number of training sources. Hyperparameters maximize mean unweighted validation AUROC within source, then equally across folds; pooled-source AUROC is not the tuning objective. Within each GEO source, models were evaluated with five donor-separated outer folds and five inner folds. The two directional transfers tune in the training source and score the other source once. The final development fit uses all 381 GEO records with inner folds stratified by source and class. Model outputs, preprocessing parameters, input hashes, split hashes, convergence checks, and warning logs are saved before external scoring. The implementation uses glum 3.4.1 with the registered solver, KKT threshold, and one fixed retry; a persistently invalid fit invalidates its dependent analysis instead of being dropped from an average.

### Development diagnostics and sensitivity analyses

Recorded technical metadata were audited without fitting expression models: title-derived chip proxies in PROBE and recorded batch in GENEPARK. A batch-only diagnostic assigns each held-out GENEPARK participant the PD fraction for that batch in the corresponding outer-training fold. For a within-batch descriptive AUROC, saved held-out M4/M5 predictions are compared only among PD-control pairs from the same recorded batch, using the eligibility threshold and aggregation rule documented before calculation. These diagnostics do not make batch a covariate in M1–M5. A separate full-cohort prevalence-only batch or plate AUROC is an explicitly in-sample metadata description.

Prespecified GEO sensitivities remove X/Y genes, residualize gene ranks against age and sex using training partitions alone, remove the union of six panel gene lists from M5's candidate universe *before* MAD selection, and construct age/sex-matched populations with fixed fold restrictions. Panel scores and M4 stay on the original full-gene representation in the panel-exclusion analysis. A partial ComBat audit uses recorded technical groups and the rank-valued expression input. Inductive arms estimate adjustment from training data without using held-out labels; the training-only disease-preserving arm includes disease in training adjustment, and the separate transductive arm uses held-out expression but no held-out disease labels. Small-batch rules, incomplete partitions, and correction warnings are reported. None of these analyses changes the primary locked model or endpoint.

### External evaluation and controls [prospective until the second lock]

The primary external comparison is `AUROC(M5) - AUROC(M4)` in the finalized PPMI population, using the frozen GEO-trained models and an inductive transformation. If the GEO final fit selected the no-gene branch or an entirely zero gene block, M5 is exactly M4 and the report says that genes were not selected in development; a zero difference in that branch is not evidence excluding useful gene information. Otherwise, a 95% percentile interval will use 5,000 paired, diagnosis-stratified donor-bootstrap resamples and common indices for both models. The interval conditions on the developed models and does not include training uncertainty. AUROC, AUPRC with case fraction, and descriptive calibration will accompany the comparison. The prespecified research-relevance margin is 0.05; it is not a clinical-utility threshold.

A GEO-control-trained five-Y-marker ridge-logistic model must first show sex-transfer AUROC at least 0.95 in each GEO direction; in PPMI controls its required AUROC is at least 0.95 with a bootstrap interval lower bound at least 0.90. Sex predictions do not overwrite recorded sex or determine identity. An autosomal ridge age predictor is a nonstopping transfer control. Available CBC measurements and longitudinal similarity are descriptive checks with the registered population, timing, and reporting limits. PPMI control scores, cell-marker scores, and disease scores will not be calculated until the final population, frozen artifacts, and public second lock are complete.
